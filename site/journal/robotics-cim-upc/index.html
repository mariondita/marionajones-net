Autonomous & Collaborative Robotics

A 40-hour course at CIM UPC on how robots work, from sensors and motors to cobots

This autumn I went back to school. From September to November 2026, two evenings a week, I took Robótica Autónoma y Colaborativa, a 40-hour course at CIM UPC, the Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya’s centre for industrial technologies in Barcelona.

We started with the fundamentals: what makes a robot autonomous, and the vocabulary to talk about it. Then we opened the robots up, from the mechanical parts, motors and cables to the sensors and the CPU, to see how all the pieces work together.

We also learned how collaborative robots, or cobots, are configured and programmed. Unlike the caged industrial arms in factories, cobots are designed to work safely side by side with people. The plan was to try it all on a real cobot in the lab, but sadly the robot was broken, a good reminder that even robots need a little care sometimes.

It feels like closing a loop that began at my family’s textile factory, watching the knitting machines run all day: robots taking over the repetitive tasks, so people are free to do the creative, thoughtful work.
