The Women’s Center

Research, information architecture & design for a domestic violence shelter’s website

By Mariona N. Jones

The Women’s Center in Carbondale, Illinois, opened in 1972 as one of the first domestic violence shelters in the United States. It offers emergency shelter, a 24-hour crisis hotline and free counseling for survivors of domestic violence and sexual assault across Southern Illinois. Today it is known as the Survivor Empowerment Center.

I designed their website, and before any design I did the research and the information architecture. A website for a shelter has very different users from most: someone in danger, often in a hurry, sometimes on a computer the person hurting them can check. Every decision started from that.

So the essentials come first. The 24-hour hotline sits at the top of every page, where it’s the first thing you see. A Safety alert explains that computer use can be monitored and that browsing history is hard to erase completely, and points to a phone number instead. An Escape button, always in the same place, takes you straight away from the site with a single click.

The rest of the content is organized around what different people come to do. Survivors find how the center can help. Volunteers and donors find how to get involved, with memorial cards, planned giving, the building fund and a needs list. Everyone else finds statistics, newsletters, success stories and an art gallery. The navigation is split in two: the main menu across the top for the center’s story, and a side menu for help, volunteering and giving, so each group can reach what they need in one or two clicks.

The tone is calm and warm on purpose: soft blues and greens, a simple house as the logo, and plain, direct language throughout.
