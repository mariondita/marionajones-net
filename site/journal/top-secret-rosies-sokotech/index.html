Top Secret Rosies

November 20, 2026 | 3 MIN READ

Mariona N. Jones
A free online course to bring more women into artificial intelligence.

SokoTech & IDEAI-UPC, 2021

In the 1940s, six women programmed ENIAC, one of the first general-purpose computers: Betty Holberton, Jean Jennings Bartik, Frances Spence, Ruth Teitelbaum, Marilyn Meltzer and Kathleen Antonelli. For decades, their work was left out of the official story. Top Secret Rosies is named in their honour.

In 2021, at SokoTech, together with IDEAI, the artificial intelligence research centre of the Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya (UPC), we launched Top Secret Rosies: a free online AI course for women studying or recently graduated in STEAM, and for any woman curious about how AI is changing our society and economy. Fundación Formación y Futuro and HP joined us as partners, with the support of Barcelona Activa and many Catalan research institutions.

Why? Women are still a small minority in technology, and even fewer of us reach decision-making roles. In AI the gap is even wider. If the people building these systems don't reflect society, the systems won't either. We wanted to open the door to quality AI jobs, in research and in industry, and to create role models for the next generation.

The course came with its own learning platform, with a content repository and a forum for the participants, and a Challenge: companies set real problems for the students to solve. HP asked for image-recognition ideas to help people with visual impairments, and the industrial AI company IRIS proposed classifying high-resolution images. The winners received their diplomas and prizes at a ceremony at Palau Robert, and we presented the project at the AI & Big Data Congress.
